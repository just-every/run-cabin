# Provider custody and authority

SECURITY-001 adds owner-managed connection grants in Channel calendars → Your data & connected providers. Before authorization the product lists each destination, purpose, shared data, provider costs and required Cabin actions. The owner selects permitted actions and homes, confirms that disclosure and can change the grant or revoke it. Credential forms for Seam, Twilio and Booking.com store AES-GCM ciphertext tied to the installation secret and provider. Rotation replaces the full credential, never returns its value, and keeps history. Revocation removes saved ciphertext and overrides any remaining deployment binding. The old credential must also be revoked at the provider.

Stripe, AI and Pantry retain their existing sealed credential editors. Provider-side scopes must also be restricted to required account/devices/operations. Cabin's saved scopes further constrain use; they do not change the provider account itself. Saving is explicitly distinct from proving a live connection. Existing explicit deployment bindings and product connection setup retain their authority until an owner saves a connection policy. An owner can authorize a scoped deployment credential without copying it into product storage. A saved disabled policy never falls back to that binding.

Account-wide reads and configuration checks require an all-homes grant. Booking.com's shared inbound queue is account-wide; choose all homes for `message:receive`, or leave polling off. A property-specific send still requires that property's grant. Provider calls read the latest grant and disconnect/restore fence at the network boundary. Already submitted operations cannot be recalled; their original outcome still needs reconciliation.

AI receives only the matched reservation's safe dates/address, approved instructions and operational sources, plus at most twelve redacted thread messages. Active door codes, stay links, key-shaped credentials, tax IDs and card/bank numbers are withheld. Message source baselines retain access revisions instead of capabilities, so rotating access still invalidates an old approval. Automatic replies remain rendered from human-approved sources under the existing property policy; model text and guest instructions cannot authorize financial, access or safety actions. Provider failure bodies are not stored or returned by the modified adapters.

## Human access and portable records

The ordinary state and bearer API responses remove active access codes, private links, credentials and sensitive free text. Signed-in people with the corresponding native authority explicitly read `/api/cabin/access` for their permitted codes, configured calendar URLs/export links, agreement links and access-bearing guest messages. Cleaners receive only their own assigned access; bearer credentials are refused. These reads are audited. The normal product's access and guest-thread screens retain their existing native authorization.

Structured archives omit installation credentials and active capabilities, and remove legacy serialized AI source baselines. A restored installation keeps its existing sharing/review fences. Rotation and disconnection cannot recall a request already submitted; original uncertain outcomes remain visible and require provider reconciliation before another write.

`tests/security.test.mjs` uses public product doors with fakes only at the paid/network edge. It proves owner-only configuration, scoped authority, encryption, stale/forged/repeated callbacks, old-key refusal, adversarial text, exact approved sending, queued revocation and private ordinary outputs. This establishes custody and authorization, not live OTA messaging, SMS registration, physical locks or processor acceptance.
